# Week 8: Comparative Analysis of Asian American Digital Pedagogies
## 1. Pedagogical Models: Chronological Timeline vs. Thematic Multimedia Textbook
| Feature | *A Different Asian American Timeline* (`aatimeline.com`) | *Foundations and Futures* (`foundationsandfutures.org`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin & Creator** | Developed by ChangeLab (grassroots political lab). | Developed by UCLA Asian American Studies Center. |
| **Core Organizing Logic** | Chronological timeline (1441 to present) organized by **Land**, **Labor**, and **Empire**. | Thematic units + 14 distinct ethnic community chapters (modules 1-5 each). |
| **Scope & Starting Point** | Starts in **1441** with the trans-Atlantic slave trade and colonization of the Americas to show racial capitalism. | Starts with thematic foundations of migration, war, empire, and community-specific histories. |
| **Pedagogical Function** | Shows relational racial formation: Asian migration cannot be separated from Black enslavement, Indigenous dispossession, and US empire. | In-depth modular textbook with primary documents, videos, oral histories, and community self-determination narratives. |
| **Format / Interaction** | Interactive horizontal timeline with visual pop-ups, broadsides, and logic overlays. | Chapter-based website with embedded video lectures, photo archives, lesson plans, and discussion questions. |

## 2. Event Comparison Case Studies
### Case Study: Chinese Exclusion Act (1882)
#### A. How *A Different Asian American Timeline* Frames It:
- **Year 1882** [Logic: `empire`]: The Chinese Exclusion Act passes. The act is initially intended to last for 10 years but is renewed in 1892 and made permanent in 1902. It is the first law to prevent a specific ethnic group from immigrating to the United States.
- **Year 1906** [Logic: `empire`]: The Japanese and Korean Exclusion League forms on the West Coast as an increasing number of Japanese and Koreans migrate from Hawai'i. San Francisco Board of Supervisors orders the segregation of all Chinese, Japanese, and Korean children into the city’s Oriental School, triggering a diplomatic crisis between the United States and Japan. A fire in San Francisco after an earthquake destroys city birth records, opening the way for more migration of Chinese “paper sons.”
- **Year 1910** [Logic: `empire`]: Angel Island immigration station opens in San Francisco Bay, mainly to enforce Chinese exclusion laws.

### Case Study: Executive Order 9066 & Japanese American Incarceration (1942)
#### A. How *A Different Asian American Timeline* Frames It:
- **Year 1900** [Logic: `land`]: Over 12,000 Japanese immigrants—many just released from indentured labor—arrive at the U.S. mainland, following Hawai’i’s annexation in 1898.
- **Year 1903** [Logic: `labor`]: In Oxnard, California, more than 1,200 Mexican and Japanese farm workers organize the first farm worker union, the Japanese-Mexican Labor Association (JMLA). It would become the first union to win a strike against the California agricultural industry, which had become a powerful force.
- **Year 1905** [Logic: `land`]: Korea, under pressure from Japan, prohibits emigration, so that Koreans don't compete against Japanese workers in Hawai'i.

### Case Study: 1968 Third World Liberation Front (TWLF) & Creation of Asian American
#### A. How *A Different Asian American Timeline* Frames It:
- **Year 1953** [Logic: `empire`]: Chinese-American artist and writer Jade Snow Wong embarks on goodwill tour of Asia under the auspices of the U.S. State Department. Several other notable Asian Americans participate in goodwill tours throughout Asia during this time, essentially serving as Cold War cultural diplomats.
- **Year 1956** [Logic: `empire`]: FBI Director J. Edgar Hoover initiates COINTELPRO, the FBI's Counterintelligence Program, designed to "increase factionalism, cause disruption and win defections” inside the Communist Party U.S.A. The program is later expanded to include disruption of the Socialist Workers Party (1961), the Ku Klux Klan (1964), the Black Panther Party and the Nation of Islam (1967), and the entire New Left, including community and religious groups (1968).
- **Year 1956** [Logic: `empire`]: Punjabi Sikh Dalip Singh Saund, of the 29th District in Los Angeles, is the first Asian American elected to Congress.

### Case Study: Vincent Chin & Pan-Asian Anti-Violence Movement (1982)
#### A. How *A Different Asian American Timeline* Frames It:
- **Year 1784** [Logic: `empire`]: The first U.S. trading ship arrives in Calcutta. Voyage of the <i>Empress of China</i> commences U.S.–China trade.
- **Year 1828** [Logic: `empire`]: The first presidential election in which non-property-holding white males could vote in the vast majority of states. By the end of the 1820s, attitudes and state laws had shifted in favor universal white male suffrage. Voter turnout soared in the 1830s, reaching about 80% of the adult white male population in the 1840 presidential election.
- **Year 1849** [Logic: `empire`]: Britain annexes the Punjab region of India. Merchant John Peters opens the Great Chinese Museum in New York City, an exhibition of Chinese curiosities—including actual Chinese people.

### Case Study: Transcontinental Railroad & 1867 Chinese Strike
#### A. How *A Different Asian American Timeline* Frames It:
- **Year 1867** [Logic: `labor`]: 
- **Year 1869** [Logic: `land`]: 
- **Year 1894** [Logic: `labor`]: Pullman Strike. The Pullman company had cut factory wages by about 25% the previous year, but continued to charge workers the same rent. If a Pullman worker went into debt, it was taken from his paycheck. On May 11,1894, 3,000 workers go on a "wildcat" strike. Many of them belonged to the American Railroad Union (ARU) founded by Eugene V. Debs. Within weeks, 50,000 workers would quit their jobs. Debs and the ARU call a massive boycott against all trains that carried a Pullman car. It would affect most rail lines west of Detroit, and, at its peak, would involve some 250,000 workers in 27 states. President Cleveland would send federal troops into Chicago, eventually breaking the strike.

### Case Study: Delano Grape Strike & Larry Itliong (1965)
#### A. How *A Different Asian American Timeline* Frames It:
- **Year 1965** [Logic: `labor`]: In California, the mostly Filipino members of the Agricultural Workers’ Organizing Committee convene at Delano’s Filipino Community Hall and vote to go on strike against Delano’s table grape growers. The Delano Grape Strike is led by Larry Itliong, who had formed AWOC in 1959 along with fellow Filipino labor leaders Philip Vera Cruz and Pete Velasco. Itliong asks the National Farm Workers Association, led by Cesar Chavez and Dolores Huerta, to join the strike. The following year, the two unions merge and become the United Farm Workers, with Chavez as director and Itliong as assistant director. The UFW would become the largest and most important farm worker union in the nation.
